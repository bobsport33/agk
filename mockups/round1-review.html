It introduces Anastasia as one person, gives creative and book visitors explicit destinations, and keeps freelance work visible without adding a third competing identity to the navigation.

How the audience structure works

  • Creative hiring: Creative work → existing campaign case studies and résumé.
  • Freelance clients: Creative work → Found Fable Creative Co. → a brand/creative inquiry.
  • Book visitors: Books → Bertha, author introduction, forthcoming projects → a books/events inquiry.
  • Editorial commissions: freelance articles belong under Creative work. Exact publication links are still needed.

What was preserved and what is proposed

The live site supplies the author copy, book description, publisher, release date, portrait, campaign imagery and contact introduction. The supplied brand system supplies Bricolage Grotesque, DM Sans, coral, teal, limoncello, warm white and near-black. The short bridge “Brand storytelling. Children’s books.” joins the two descriptors from your sitemap. Navigation, audience-specific inquiry controls and layout are proposed UI.

Source conflicts resolved for this review

  • Your four-link navigation takes precedence over the handoff’s older Portfolio / Books / Found Fable navigation.
  • The handoff calls books work in progress. The current live Books page now supplies October 3, 2027 and Bushel & Peck Books; those details are used here.
  • The handoff references an App Router build. The repository currently uses the Pages Router. These mockups do not require a router migration.
  • The live site’s current logo is used as a raster reference. The repository SVG is an older mark; request the current vector file for production.

Content and asset gaps before production

  • Final Bertha cover, illustrator credits, book metadata, and confirmed purchase or preorder destination. The archival photo is explicitly identified as temporary artwork.
  • Article titles, publication names, dates and URLs. The phrase “time articles” is ambiguous; no TIME publication claim has been invented.
  • Approved service descriptions and any inquiry-routing email addresses. The contact form is a local demonstration; it sends nothing.
  • School/library visit details, age range, availability, event format and press materials, if these are services to offer.
  • Current SVG logo and approved client-logo strip. The prototypes use client names and existing campaign images.
